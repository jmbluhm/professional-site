# Maker Mindset

## Activity Context

Through activities like laser cutting and prototyping, Jordan explores hands-on creation, rapid iteration, and craftsmanship.

## Skills Developed

### Hands-On Creation

Physical making provides direct feedback and requires translating ideas into tangible reality. This develops:
- Attention to detail
- Understanding of constraints
- Problem-solving in physical space
- Quality consciousness

### Rapid Iteration

Making activities encourage quick iteration cycles:
- Try an approach
- Observe results
- Adjust and try again
- Learn from failures

### Craftsmanship

Physical making develops appreciation for quality, precision, and the details that distinguish adequate work from excellent work.

## Relevance to Product Work

These maker activities translate directly to product development:

### Prototyping Mindset

Comfort with building quick prototypes to test ideas before committing to full implementation.

### Iterative Refinement

Understanding that good products emerge through iteration rather than perfect initial design.

### Quality Standards

Developing high standards for product quality and attention to detail that improves user experience.

### Experimentation

Willingness to experiment with new approaches and learn from both successes and failures.

## Professional Application

The maker mindset supports product experimentation and execution, encouraging a bias toward building and testing rather than extended planning and analysis.

## Context

This represents a professionally relevant personal interest that reinforces skills directly applicable to product management work, particularly in building prototypes and maintaining high quality standards.
