# AI Product Strategy

## Strategic Philosophy

Jordan believes AI products should be platform-enabled rather than isolated point solutions, designed with safety and governance from the start, and closely integrated with core product workflows.

## Platform-First Approach

### Centralized Infrastructure

AI capabilities should be built on shared infrastructure that multiple features can leverage. This approach provides:
- Consistent behavior across features
- Reduced duplication of effort
- Centralized governance and monitoring
- Faster development of new AI features

### Reusable Components

Platform thinking emphasizes building reusable components that teams can combine in different ways rather than building complete solutions from scratch each time.

## Safety and Governance

### Design-Time Considerations

Safety and governance requirements should be incorporated during initial design, not added later. This includes:
- Data access controls
- Audit logging
- Permission boundaries
- Compliance requirements
- Error handling and fallbacks

### Enterprise Requirements

Enterprise customers require higher standards for security, compliance, and reliability. AI products must meet these standards from the beginning rather than retrofitting them later.

## Integration with Core Workflows

### Workflow-Native AI

AI features should integrate seamlessly into existing user workflows rather than requiring users to adopt new tools or processes.

### Value Delivery

AI should enhance existing product value rather than being positioned as a separate capability. Users should experience AI as a natural extension of the product, not as a distinct feature.

## Avoiding Hype-Driven Decisions

Jordan emphasizes building AI products based on genuine user needs rather than market hype. This means:
- Validating that AI solves real problems
- Measuring actual impact, not just adoption
- Being honest about limitations
- Choosing appropriate technology rather than the newest technology

## Long-Term Thinking

AI strategy should balance immediate needs with long-term platform capabilities, ensuring that early decisions support rather than constrain future development.
